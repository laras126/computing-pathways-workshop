Collective action — the coordination of many people toward shared goals — is one of the defining challenges of our time. From neighborhood mutual aid to large-scale civic organizing, the tools people use to act together shape what becomes possible.

This workshop brings together researchers, designers, and practitioners to examine how HCI can better support the social, political, and infrastructural dimensions of collective action. We will explore gaps in current frameworks, exchange methods and cases from the field, and identify promising directions for future research and design.

Collective action raises questions that cut across the field: How do we design for groups rather than individuals? What does it mean to build infrastructure that communities can sustain and govern themselves? When does technology help organizing, and when does it get in the way? We welcome perspectives from community organizing, labor, mutual aid, civic technology, crisis response, and beyond.
